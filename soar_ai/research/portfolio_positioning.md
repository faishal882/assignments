# Portfolio positioning for Soar founding-engineer outreach

This is the short evidence stack Faishal should lead with when Henry asks for background. Keep it focused on Soar’s likely needs: speed, workflow reliability, internal tools, analytics, and systems depth.

## Positioning headline

Full-stack product engineer who can own the messy layer between growth, booking reliability, internal tools, analytics, and production operations.

## Proof stack

1. **Tailorec full-stack product** — built a production AI-assisted career platform spanning Next.js frontend, FastAPI backend, agent/runtime workflows, analytics, and AWS operations.
2. **External workflow reliability** — Tailorec required stateful user-facing workflows, event traces, user gates, browser/runtime automation, and operational tooling, which maps to Soar’s booking/hold/payment/support workflow shape.
3. **Growth and attribution** — built outreach/product analytics around campaign links, signup attribution, PostHog-style event flows, and acquisition-to-product reporting.
4. **Infrastructure ownership** — operated AWS deployment pieces including ECS/Fargate-style services, ALB, Cloud Map, CloudWatch, snapshots, and teardown/runbook discipline.
5. **Systems depth** — built a 32-bit OS from scratch with keyboard, mouse, VGA, ATA, memory management, multitasking, and FAT32 support.
6. **Founder-style work pattern** — local Codex/Obsidian evidence shows repeated architecture analysis, production-debugging prep, MVP narrowing, technical writeups, and founder outreach practice.

## What not to overemphasize

- Do not pitch as a generic “AI engineer”; Soar’s visible problem is booking reliability + growth + internal tooling, with AI optional.
- Do not overclaim travel-domain expertise; pitch fast learning and adjacent external-provider workflow experience.
- Do not lead with the OS project alone; use it as systems-depth proof after the product/workflow story.
- Do not send the full dossier unless asked; send the concise message, then the one-page teardown.
