# Pre-send checklist for contacting Henry

Use this immediately before sending a DM/email so the outreach stays sharp and credible.

## Before sending

- Confirm the current Soar site still matches the core read: live flight search/booking, PWA-style mobile experience, and public terms naming Duffel.
- Re-open Henry’s X profile and check whether bio, role, or pinned/recent posts changed.
- Pick one channel only for the first message; do not blast X, LinkedIn, and email at the same time.
- Use the short DM unless you have a warm intro or email context.
- Attach nothing in the first message; offer the one-page teardown only after he replies or shows interest.
- Keep the pitch centered on booking reliability + internal tooling + growth funnel, not generic AI enthusiasm.
- Avoid saying “I reverse engineered your app”; say “I studied public product/legal/client-route signals.”
- Do not claim funding, investor, team size, or private architecture facts.
- Be ready to send `henry_followup_teardown.md` within 5 minutes if he asks for the teardown.
- Be ready to answer with the 48-hour work-trial plan if he asks what you would actually ship.

## First message success criteria

The message should prove four things quickly:

1. You noticed Soar’s actual product shape.
2. You understand the hard operational layer behind a simple booking UX.
3. You have adjacent proof from Tailorec and systems work.
4. You can propose one concrete thing to own immediately.

## Follow-up timing

- If no reply after 3-5 days, send one short follow-up with a single new artifact offer.
- If still no reply, do not keep bumping; build the 48-hour artifact independently and send only if it is genuinely impressive.

## Follow-up line

“Quick bump — I can send the one-page teardown if useful. The core idea is measuring and hardening shared fare → hold → ticketed booking, plus the internal timeline console behind failed/ambiguous bookings.”
