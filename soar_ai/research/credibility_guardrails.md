# Credibility guardrails: what not to say to Henry

The research package is strongest when it is precise and humble. These guardrails prevent sounding creepy, inflated, or uninformed.

## Do not claim private knowledge

Say: “Based on public product/legal/client-route signals, my hypothesis is...”

Do not say: “Your backend is...” or “I know you use...” unless the source is explicit, such as public terms naming Duffel.

## Do not overstate funding status

Say: “I did not find a reliable public funding announcement, so I would treat funding as undisclosed.”

Do not say: “bootstrapped,” “funded,” “YC,” or name investors unless Henry or a credible public source confirms it.

## Do not over-index on reverse engineering

Say: “I studied the public product surface and routes to understand the workflow shape.”

Do not lead with “I inspected your JS bundle.” That can sound adversarial even when the source is public.

## Do not pitch generic AI

Soar’s visible wedge is not “AI for travel.” The better wedge is booking reliability, social/deal-link growth, internal tooling, support operations, and trust around external providers.

## Do not make the first ask too big

Do not ask for production access, an interview loop, or a broad founding engineer role immediately. Ask for permission to send the one-page teardown or offer a narrow 48-hour artifact.

## Do not overuse Henry’s age

His public bio says “18,” but the outreach should not make that the focus. Mention builder speed, product taste, internal tools, and app growth signals instead.

## Do not send local/private notes

Use the local Obsidian/Codex evidence only to shape your pitch and proof stack. Do not send raw private notes or conversation summaries.

## Safe default sentence

“Everything here is based on public signals, so I may be wrong on implementation details — but the workflow risks I’d want to validate are booking state, payment/verification ambiguity, support tooling, and deal-link conversion.”
